The Vision

Okere Mom-Kok

The name means "a baby should not cry." In 2019, Donnas Ojok-Okello returned to his ancestral home in Otuke District — a community still healing from the LRA insurgency — and chose to build, not leave.

No school nearby. No clinic. No clean water. Just shea trees, community resilience, and a bold plan to create Africa's first sustainable rural city.

"A community that has taken ownership of its own growth — putting education, health, and the shea tree at its center."
The Engine

Shea Butter Changed Everything

The wild Vitellaria nilotica trees of Otuke became the foundation of a new economy. Women who once had no income now lead a cooperative that exports globally.

Today, Okere City is alive — children in school, buildings being built, and women processing shea to the rhythm of traditional songs passed down through generations.
